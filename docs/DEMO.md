# Simtra — Crafting Constellations demo

## 90-second walkthrough after A and B are integrated

0–15s: Open the existing moving city. “A community looks like thousands of separate people. Simtra helps us explore the patterns connecting their perspectives.” Explain that residents are synthetic and Census-derived.

15–35s: Ask “Would San Francisco residents support more frequent public transit?” Show the live model estimate and demographic breakdowns. Do not promise a particular percentage; use the actual result.

35–55s: Open Constellations. “Each star represents a synthetic resident; the constellations illustrate shared responses.” Show group labels, weighted shares, and click a star. Explain that links are illustrative, not real friendships or independently observed individual votes.

55–75s: Compare two transit messages through the A/B composer. A: “More frequent buses mean shorter commutes and fewer cars.” B: “Reliable transit keeps the city accessible for workers, students, and families.” Show the real result and provenance. Mention Jev only if the completed result identifies the Jev path; a configured key is not proof of completion.

75–90s: Return to City. “Crafting constellations means making connections visible. Simtra turns simulated community perspectives into patterns you can explore.” End on a resident and the unchanged city.

## Before presenting

Merge both teammate branches, verify the real prediction and Jev A/B requests, test constellation/city round trips and switching cities, inspect mobile layout, and confirm no old-city result survives a population change. Treat the output as simulated estimates, not validated forecasts. Cached news and disabled memory remain limitations until separately configured.

## Short pitch

Simtra connects simulated community perspectives into constellations of shared beliefs. Explore a living city, ask a question, and see how individual synthetic personas form larger patterns—then compare messages and inspect what connects each group.
