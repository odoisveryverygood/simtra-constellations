Know the impact of a million-dollar decision in twenty minutes instead of four months.

A live simulation of San Francisco: 10,000 digital residents built from real US Census data, each with a background, motivation, memory and daily life.

1Simulate

Ask the city anything.

  • Type a question in the box at the bottom, e.g. What if I increase the price of Chipotle bowls by 20%?
  • Residents answer individually; you get the split (keep buying · buy less · switch · stop) and the reasons.
  • A/B: compare two messages with the same audience.
2Filter

Ask exactly who matters.

  • Tap the audience pill: age, neighborhood, occupation, education, e.g. 25-year-old software engineers.
  • The result shows who was asked and how many Census records back it.
3Understand

See the people behind every number.

  • Under each result: bar, histogram, scatter, box or line by demographic.
  • Hover a bar to see the residents; click it to read each person’s own answer and why; click a person for their full profile.
4Remember

The city evolves as you use it.

  • Post news or events in the Timeline (A 5.9 earthquake hit the Bay Area); residents react in character.
  • Every event and survey is remembered and shapes the next answer. Ask the same question again and the timeline shows the shift.
  • Everything you post and ask lives in the Timeline; verified Census questions (show the age distribution) work too.
Track record

2024 presidency: 83.8% actual vs 81.3% predicted. March 2024 Prop A: 70.4% vs 70%. Backtested with a model whose knowledge cutoff predates both events, so nothing was memorized.
